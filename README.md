# CS-250
ChadaTech project for CS 250 Software Development Lifecycle SNHU
## How do I interpret user needs and implement them into a program? How does creating user stories help with this?
While working through this project, I was able to practice each role of the Scrum Team and see what was needed and what the roles involved. I made sure to stay organized and keep the project vision in mind. I took the six prioritized user stories and analyzed them to understand what users needed, then broke that work into smaller steps. User stories helped me identify who needed a feature, what they wanted, and why it mattered. I used the acceptance criteria to guide development and testing so I could check whether the features met those needs. I also learned how to adjust when requirements changed and the project shifted toward wellness travel. This showed me how to revise the work while still keeping the user's expectations in mind.
## How do I approach developing programs? What Agile processes do I hope to incorporate into my future development work?
Learning about Waterfall and Agile gave me a broader view of how to approach building a system and the steps needed to make it happen. Practicing the different team roles gave me insight into the struggles each role might face and how they depend on each other to make the system better for users. I approach development by understanding the requirements, breaking the work into manageable tasks, and testing as I go. In my future development work, I would like to use sprint planning to organize smaller pieces of work, sprint reviews to gather feedback, and retrospectives to find ways to improve. Working a little at a time would help me stay organized, focus on quality, and adjust when the user's needs change.
## What does it mean to be a good team member in software development?
Clear Communication. Communication is a big part of being a good team member. I believe that clear and regular communication helps reduce misunderstandings and makes it easier to address problems early. Someone's struggles might be easier to work through with help from a teammate who has faced a similar issue before. Communicating what is needed, what problems there are, and what changes are happening keeps everyone prepared and allows the team to brainstorm solutions together. Being a good teammate also means completing my responsibilities, listening to feedback, respecting other people's ideas, and asking for help when I need it. Everyone brings different experiences, and being willing to learn from each other helps the whole team succeed.
